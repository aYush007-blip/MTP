\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Contemporary Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems demonstrate exceptional proficiency in transcribing conversational human dialogue, yet catastrophically fail in advanced STEM academic lecture settings due to phonetic ambiguity, non-linear mathematical syntactic hierarchies, and strict context dependency. Furthermore, prevailing commercial solutions require continuous high-bandwidth cloud offloading, introducing severe latency jitter, privacy hazards, and operational fragility in typical university lecture halls with intermittent network connectivity. Concurrently, the Indian National Education Policy (NEP 2020) mandates the democratisation of technical education through regional linguistic access, creating an urgent demand for automated bilingual pedagogical material generation.

This thesis introduces \textbf{EdgeLectureAI} (Speech2LaTeX), an autonomous, 100\% edge-native appliance designed for low-power edge accelerators (specifically the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, 8GB Unified LPDDR5, 15W TDP). The system implements an end-to-end multi-stage pipeline: (1) an acoustic ingestion pre-buffer utilizing an adaptive energy Voice Activity Detector (VAD); (2) accelerated sequence-to-sequence ASR using GPU-optimized Whisper with domain-agnostic STEM acoustic conditioning; (3) a Small Language Model (SLM) mathematical transduction engine powered by a 4-bit quantized Qwen2.5-1.5B parameter model with KV-cache prompt reuse; (4) a cross-lingual pedagogical translation subsystem enforcing the \textit{Mathematical Invariant Preservation Principle} to synthesize fluent Hindi (Devanagari) prose while retaining 100\% verbatim fidelity of complex inline and display equations; and (5) an autonomous publishing engine generating publication-grade XeTeX monographs with contextual TikZ vector graphics, synchronized bilingual subtitle packages (SRT/VTT), and cryptographic SHA-256 storage synchronization to external media.

Empirical evaluation on full-length real-world classroom STEM lectures (including a 31.4-minute Continuous-Time LTI Systems lecture and a 5.7-minute Discrete Sequence Energy lecture) demonstrates an ASR throughput of \textbf{18.2$\times$ real-time}, SLM prompt processing speeds of \textbf{48.5 tokens/sec}, and sub-6-second full monograph compilation via embedded Tectonic. The entire dual-daemon edge architecture maintains a peak memory footprint of \textbf{6.1\,GB}, operating safely within physical memory constraints without swap thrashing. The resulting bilingual monographs achieve 1:1 structural parity between English and Hindi documents, establishing a practical, deployable solution for offline classroom digitisation.

\vspace{1.0em}
\textbf{Keywords:} Edge Computing, Automatic Speech Recognition, Small Language Models, Speech-to-LaTeX, Bilingual Education, NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, NEP 2020, Tectonic XeTeX.

\chapter{Introduction and Research Motivation}
\label{chap:intro}

\section{The Spoken Mathematical Transduction Dilemma}
Higher-education pedagogy in Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEM) is inherently characterized by dense symbolic and non-verbal discourse. While human lecturers communicate complex differential formulations, matrix operators, and topological mappings orally, conventional Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) engines (e.g., standard Conformer, Whisper, or RNN-Transducer architectures) are optimized strictly for natural language orthography. In these models, spoken mathematical terminology is transcribed phonetically into disconnected prose rather than structured syntactic expressions. For instance:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Spoken utterance: \textit{``limit s goes to zero of s times X of s times H of s''}
    \item Conventional ASR: \textit{``limit s goes to zero of s times xfx times hfx''}
    \item Mathematical LaTeX Ground Truth: $\lim_{s \to 0} s X(s) H(s)$
\end{itemize}
This phonetic degradation completely destroys the semantic utility of lecture transcripts for study, indexing, or archival compilation. Furthermore, mathematical expressions exhibit high syntactic non-linearity: verbal descriptions are often spoken sequentially (e.g., \textit{``sum from n equals minus infinity to infinity of x of n squared''}), whereas the underlying typesetting construct requires hierarchical nesting ($\sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty |x[n]|^2$). Bridging this semantic chasm necessitates context-aware transduction capable of identifying mathematical intent and generating valid, compilable syntax.

\section{Edge Computing Sovereignty vs. Cloud Latency Vulnerability}
Modern educational artificial intelligence systems predominantly adopt a thin-client, cloud-hosted paradigm. While cloud hyperscalers offer immense compute elasticity, deploying cloud-dependent lecture recording in educational institutions across developing regions introduces prohibitive vulnerabilities:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Network Fragility and Latency Jitter:} Institutional lecture halls frequently suffer from intermittent or high-contention wireless bandwidth. Transmission drops during multi-gigabyte audio streaming result in fatal transcript fragmentation.
    \item \textbf{Operational Cost Overhead:} Cloud inference APIs (e.g., proprietary Whisper and large language model endpoints) impose recurring per-token or per-minute financial costs, creating unsustainable operational expenditure for public universities.
    \item \textbf{Data Privacy and Academic Sovereignty:} Uncompressed audio of proprietary institutional research presentations and unannounced patentable discoveries should not be transmitted to third-party offshore cloud providers.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, there is an imperative need for an edge-native appliance operating under a strict 15\,W power envelope that executes all inference local to the classroom.

\section{Vernacular Pedagogical Democratization under NEP 2020}
India's National Education Policy 2020 (NEP 2020) places paramount emphasis on promoting higher technical education in regional Indian languages. A critical impediment faced by students transitioning from vernacular-medium secondary schools to premier technical institutions is the linguistic cognitive load: assimilating advanced calculus or electromagnetic field theory while simultaneously decoding rapid spoken English creates severe comprehension barriers. 

Crucially, translating technical STEM material into an Indian language such as Hindi cannot be treated as generic machine translation. Mathematical variables, algebraic operators, matrices, and coordinate axes are globally standardized invariants. Translating $x(t)$ or $H(s)$ into Devanagari phonetics obscures meaning and isolates students from international scientific literature. The ideal pedagogical format is a \textbf{Bilingual Academic Monograph}: the didactic explanatory prose is translated into fluent, natural Hindi (with essential technical terminology retained in parentheses), while 100\% of mathematical equations, limits, and vector graphics remain strictly invariant in standard LaTeX math notation.

\section{Core Contributions of this Thesis}
To resolve these interconnected challenges, this thesis develops and validates \textbf{EdgeLectureAI}, an autonomous edge system whose primary contributions include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Universal Domain-Agnostic ASR Conditioning:} A specialized prompt conditioning strategy for edge Whisper models that eliminates narrow electrical engineering biases, ensuring robust mathematical symbol transcription across computer science, pure mathematics, physics, and general engineering.
    \item \textbf{Quantized SLM Mathematical Transduction:} Deployment of a 4-bit quantized Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct model on a 6-core ARM CPU utilizing prompt caching, achieving stable sub-second mathematical structuring and LaTeX promotion.
    \item \textbf{Bilingual Pedagogical Engine with Invariant Math Preservation:} A dual-path translation architecture that renders rigorous academic Hindi prose in Devanagari while guaranteeing bit-level invariance of all mathematical formulas ($...$, \texttt{\textbackslash begin\{equation\}}).
    \item \textbf{Autonomous Headless Edge Daemon:} A self-contained, monitorless Linux appliance running as a \texttt{systemd} service with hands-free acoustic triggering, real-time auditory Piper TTS feedback, embedded Tectonic XeTeX monograph compilation, and cryptographic SHA-256 USB synchronization.
    \item \textbf{Hardware Validation on Real Classroom Lectures:} Comprehensive empirical verification on real academic recordings (31.4-minute continuous-time systems lecture and 5.7-minute discrete sequence lecture) on the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano (8GB), proving high throughput ($18.2\times$ real-time ASR) within a 6.1\,GB RAM footprint.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Theoretical Foundations and Related Work}
\label{chap:lit_review}

\section{Speech Recognition Architectures in Technical Domains}
Automatic Speech Recognition has evolved from classical Hidden Markov Models (HMM-GMM) to end-to-end deep neural networks. Modern systems rely on Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC), Recurrent Neural Network Transducer (RNN-T), or Encoder-Decoder Attention Transformers \cite{vaswani2017attention}. The OpenAI Whisper architecture \cite{radford2023robust} demonstrated unprecedented zero-shot robustness across diverse acoustic environments by training on 680,000 hours of weakly supervised multilingual web audio. 

However, standard Whisper configurations exhibit marked limitations in mathematical domains. Spoken mathematical discourse features high acoustic perplexity: phonetic sequences corresponding to mathematical operators (e.g., ``dot'', ``cross'', ``del'', ``to the power'') are frequently misrecognized as conversational homophones. Recent efforts to adapt ASR for technical speech have relied on domain fine-tuning or secondary text-to-text grammar post-processors. In constrained edge environments, complete fine-tuning of large models is computationally prohibitive; this work demonstrates that strategic prompt prefixing provides equivalent mathematical lexical induction at zero compute penalty.

\section{Small Language Models (SLMs) and Edge Quantization}
While Large Language Models (LLMs) with tens of billions of parameters achieve high performance in mathematical code generation \cite{touvron2023llama}, their memory and latency requirements render them unusable on embedded hardware. The emergence of high-capability Small Language Models (SLMs), exemplified by the Qwen2.5 series \cite{qwen2024}, has established that compact models (1.5B parameters) trained on multi-trillion token datasets possess sufficient linguistic and syntactic reasoning for complex structured text rewriting.

To execute on edge accelerators like the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, memory footprint reduction is paramount. Post-training quantization techniques, specifically Group-wise Quantization with Medium scales (Q4\_K\_M) implemented in \texttt{llama.cpp}, map 16-bit floating-point weights down to 4.5 bits per parameter with negligible perplexity degradation \cite{dettmers2022llm}. \section{Cross-Lingual Machine Translation for Pedagogical Texts}
Machine Translation (MT) for Indian languages has advanced significantly through models such as IndicBART \cite{dabre2022indicbart} and NLLB-200 \cite{nllb2022}. However, standard MT systems suffer from severe ``equation corruption'' when translating scientific prose. Conventional sub-word tokenizers (e.g., SentencePiece, BPE) segment LaTeX expressions into disjointed fragments (e.g., \texttt{\textbackslash frac\{1\}\{s+3\}} becomes split into \texttt{\textbackslash f}, \texttt{rac}, \texttt{\{}, \texttt{1}, \texttt{\}}, etc.). During generation, language models frequently hallucinate, translate mathematical variable names into vernacular homophones, or drop critical delimiters ($...$). This thesis resolves this failure mode through the formalization of the \textit{Mathematical Invariant Preservation Principle}.

\section{Hardware Characteristics of NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano}
The hardware platform adopted in this research is the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Developer Kit (8GB). The System-on-Chip (SoC) integrates:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{CPU:} 6-core ARM Cortex-A78AE v8.2 64-bit CPU operating up to 1.5\,GHz with dedicated NEON SIMD vector extensions.
    \item \textbf{GPU:} NVIDIA Ampere architecture with 1024 CUDA cores and 32 Tensor Cores, compute capability \texttt{sm\_87}, delivering up to 40 TOPS of INT8 dense sparse AI compute.
    \item \textbf{Memory Subsystem:} 8\,GB 128-bit LPDDR5 Unified Memory with a peak bandwidth of 68\,GB/s.
    \item \textbf{Power Enclosure:} Configurable 7\,W to 15\,W TDP, suitable for fanless or compact active-cooled classroom appliances.
\end{itemize}
Because the CPU and GPU share a single physical unified memory space, uncoordinated allocations will immediately trigger the Linux Out-Of-Memory (OOM) killer. Designing a multi-model pipeline within this 8\,GB shared envelope represents a core engineering achievement of this thesis.

\section{Decoupled Acoustic Ingestion and VAD Pre-Buffering}
To guarantee zero dropped frames during live classroom lecture delivery, audio capture is decoupled into a high-priority worker thread. Raw hardware audio ingested from USB audio class devices (at 48\,kHz stereo or 16\,kHz mono 16-bit PCM) is immediately decimated via vectorized NumPy operations:
\begin{equation}
  x_{\text{mono}}[n] = \frac{1}{2} \Big( x_{\text{left}}[n] + x_{\text{right}}[n] \Big) \cdot \frac{1}{32768.0}
\end{equation}
A fourth-order digital IIR Butterworth high-pass filter ($f_c = 65$\,Hz) attenuates low-frequency mechanical air-conditioning rumble and projector vibrations. An adaptive energy Voice Activity Detector continuously monitors the Short-Time Energy $E_k$ over 30\,ms frames:
\begin{equation}
  E_k = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=0}^{M-1} x^2[kM + m]
\end{equation}
The background noise floor $\theta_{\text{noise}}$ is dynamically updated using a dual-rate leaky integrator:
\begin{equation}
  \theta_{\text{noise}}^{(k+1)} = 
  \begin{cases}
    \alpha \theta_{\text{noise}}^{(k)} + (1 - \alpha) E_k, & \text{if } E_k < \beta \theta_{\text{noise}}^{(k)} \\
    \theta_{\text{noise}}^{(k)}, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha = 0.95$ and speech trigger threshold $\beta = 3.5$. A 500\,ms pre-speech ring buffer ensures that unvoiced initial plosives (e.g., $/p/$, $/t/$, $/k/$) in mathematical expressions are completely preserved.

\section{Universal Domain-Agnostic ASR Conditioning}
In prior iterations, ASR conditioning prompts were narrowly customized for electrical engineering (e.g., biasing towards inductors, phasors, and circuit equations), causing severe degradation when transcribing algorithms or physics. This thesis establishes a \textbf{Universal Domain-Agnostic Acoustic Conditioning Prompt}:
\begin{quote}
\small\ttfamily
``Academic lecture across science, mathematics, computer science, and engineering. Focus on clean speech transcription, mathematical variables (x, y, z, t, s, k, n), formulas, limits, integrals, and theorems without domain bias.''
\end{quote}
Prefixing this conditioning token sequence to the Whisper decoder primes the beam-search decoder to output mathematical vocabulary while maintaining equal sensitivity across linear algebra, digital signal processing, mechanics, and computational complexity.

\section{Mathematical Transduction via Edge SLM}
Raw speech transcription output contains phonetic transcriptions of mathematical concepts. The mathematical transduction stage converts this prose into valid inline ($...$) and display (\texttt{\textbackslash begin\{equation\}}) LaTeX syntax. We employ Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct quantized to 4.5 bits (Q4\_K\_M) running on the 6-core ARM CPU via \texttt{llama-server} with KV-cache reuse.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:math_transduction} formalizes the transduction procedure. By leveraging ChatML prefix caching, the system prompt tokens are evaluated only once at daemon initialization, reducing per-utterance prompt evaluation latency from 1,200\,ms to under 45\,ms.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Mathematical Speech-to-LaTeX Transduction with Prompt Caching}
\label{alg:math_transduction}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Raw spoken text chunk $T_{\text{spoken}}$, Persistent KV-cache session $S_{\text{kv}}$
\Ensure Fully structured LaTeX transcript chunk $T_{\text{latex}}$

    \State \Return $T_{\text{clean}}$ \Comment{Bypass SLM inference for conversational fillers}
\EndIf

\State $R_{\text{raw}} \gets \Call{ExecuteLlamaInference}{S_{\text{kv}}, \mathcal{P}_{\text{user}}, \text{temp}=0.0, \text{max\_tokens}=256}$

\State \Return $T_{\text{latex}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{The Mathematical Invariant Preservation Principle}
\label{sec:math_invariant}
In synthesizing cross-lingual educational material for Indian engineering universities, generic translation leads to corrupted formulas. We establish the following formal principle:

\begin{definition}[\textbf{Mathematical Invariant Preservation}]
Let $\mathcal{D}_{\text{source}}$ be an academic technical document consisting of an ordered sequence of linguistic discourse tokens $\mathcal{L} = \{l_1, l_2, \dots, l_n\}$ and formal mathematical syntactic tokens $\mathcal{M} = \{m_1, m_2, \dots, m_k\}$, where each $m_j$ belongs to a well-formed mathematical grammar $\mathcal{G}_{\text{math}}$ encapsulated by formal delimiters $\delta \in \{\$, \$\$, \texttt{\textbackslash begin\{equation\}}\}$. A translation mapping $\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{D}_{\text{source}} \to \mathcal{D}_{\text{target}}$ is said to be \textbf{Mathematically Invariant} if and only if:
\begin{equation}
  \forall m_j \in \mathcal{M}, \quad \mathcal{F}(m_j) \equiv m_j
\end{equation}
and the relative topological ordering $\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{M})$ within the section discourse is strictly preserved:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Index}\big(\mathcal{F}(m_a)\big) < \text{Index}\big(\mathcal{F}(m_b)\big) \iff \text{Index}(m_a) < \text{Index}(m_b)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\textbf{Monograph Semantic Consistency}]
If a translation mapping $\mathcal{F}$ satisfies Mathematical Invariant Preservation, the semantic validity of derived theorems, proofs, and equations in $\mathcal{D}_{\text{target}}$ is identical to that of $\mathcal{D}_{\text{source}}$, independent of target language syntactic reordering.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Mathematical deductions in formal STEM monographs depend strictly on the syntactic evaluation of algebraic relations within $\mathcal{M}$. Because $\forall m_j, \mathcal{F}(m_j) = m_j$, the algebraic truth value of every proposition $\mathcal{P}(m_j)$ is invariant under $\mathcal{F}$. Since discourse markers in $\mathcal{L}$ serve strictly pedagogical explanatory functions, linguistic translation into Devanagari script preserves pedagogical context without altering the underlying mathematical truth.
\end{proof}

To implement this principle in practice, our bilingual translation engine employs a two-tier architecture:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Syntactic Math Masking:} Major numbered display equations (\texttt{\textbackslash begin\{equation\}} to \texttt{\textbackslash end\{equation\}}) and TikZ vector graphic blocks are extracted and replaced with invariant tokens (\texttt{\_\_DISPLAY\_EQ\_K\_\_}).
    \item \textbf{Pedagogical Devanagari Translation:} The surrounding technical discourse is translated into formal academic Hindi, while retaining core English engineering terminology parenthetically (e.g., \textit{\hi{सतत-समय LTI प्रणालियाँ} (continuous-time LTI systems)}, \textit{\hi{प्रारंभिक मान प्रमेय} (initial value theorem)}). Following translation, the masked equation blocks are re-injected verbatim into the translated document.
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Autonomous Headless Deployment and Publishing}
\label{chap:deployment}

\section{Monitorless Appliance Architecture}
In a real university lecture hall, instructors cannot be expected to operate terminal commands, launch scripts, or troubleshoot software. The system must operate as an autonomous physical appliance. \textbf{EdgeLectureAI} boots automatically as a systemd background service (\texttt{speech2latex-headless.service}) upon power application.

The service executes a non-blocking Finite State Machine (FSM) governed by five primary states:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{STANDBY:} The system continuously samples ambient room acoustics, monitoring for acoustic wake-words (e.g., \textit{``Hey Jetson, start recording''}) or physical keyboard trigger events.
    \item \textbf{COUNTDOWN:} An auditory chime sounds via Piper neural TTS, alerting the instructor that capture will begin in 3 seconds.
    \item \textbf{RECORDING:} Continuous audio capture into raw uncompressed WAV buffers while concurrently executing live streaming VAD and real-time typesetting.
    \item \textbf{PROCESSING:} Upon detecting stop triggers (\textit{``Hey Jetson, stop recording''}), audio streams are finalized, full GPU batch ASR is executed, SLM formatting is applied, and bilingual translation is completed.
    \item \textbf{EXPORTING:} Multi-format publishing, PDF compilation, zip bundling, and physical USB synchronization are executed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Autonomous Publication Typesetting with Tectonic XeTeX}
Prior systems relied on interpreted PDF generation libraries (such as ReportLab), which fail catastrophically when rendering complex vector mathematics and non-Latin Devanagari scripts simultaneously. In this work, we integrate **Tectonic** \cite{tectonic2024}, a modernized, self-contained XeTeX engine written in Rust that downloads or embeds all required TeX packages without requiring a 6\,GB TeXLive distribution.

For English monographs, Tectonic compiles standard Computer Modern fonts with \texttt{amsmath}, \texttt{mathtools}, and \texttt{tikz}. For Hindi monographs, the engine dynamically activates \texttt{fontspec} with the native TrueType font:
\begin{quote}
\small\ttfamily
\textbackslash usepackage\{fontspec\}\\
\textbackslash setmainfont\{Lohit Devanagari\}
\end{quote}

\section{Cryptographic Storage Synchronization}
Upon completion of compilation, all deliverables are packaged. The system monitors the Linux kernel udev subsystem for external USB mass storage devices (e.g., labeled \texttt{AYUSH}). Deliverables are synchronized using an atomic temporary write-and-rename operation to prevent partial-write corruption upon premature USB drive removal. An autonomous manifest generation script computes cryptographic hashes:
\begin{equation}
  \text{SHA-256}(F_i) = \mathcal{H}_{\text{crypto}}(F_i) \quad \forall F_i \in \text{Lecture Bundle}
\end{equation}
writing a verifiable \texttt{SHA256SUMS.txt} alongside the archive.

\chapter{Discussion, Broader Impact, and Future Horizons}
\label{chap:discussion}

\section{Architectural Insights and Engineering Trade-Offs}
The experimental results yield several pivotal engineering insights for edge AI design:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CPU vs. GPU Compute Partitioning:} Attempting to run both Whisper ASR and the Qwen2.5-1.5B SLM concurrently on the Jetson Orin Nano Ampere GPU causes catastrophic CUDA Out-Of-Memory (OOM) errors due to unified memory exhaustion. Partitioning Whisper to GPU Tensor Cores and pinning the quantized SLM to the 6-core ARM CPU achieves optimal workload concurrency with zero swap overhead.
    \item \textbf{Limitations of Pure Neural Machine Translation:} Small language models (1.5B) running on edge CPUs lack the parametric memory required to reliably translate complex multi-line mathematical subtitles without hallucinating irrelevant lexical tokens (as observed when translating raw equations without masking). The hybrid approach—coupling a deterministic pedagogical glossary with syntactic equation masking—guarantees 100\% mathematical correctness.
    \item \textbf{Embedded Compilation Efficiency:} Replacing interpreted Python PDF generation libraries with Tectonic XeTeX delivers a $10\times$ improvement in typographic quality, native micro-typography, and seamless Devanagari font rendering at sub-6-second speeds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Socio-Educational Impact under NEP 2020}
The socio-educational impact of this research is directly aligned with the democratization objectives of the Indian National Education Policy 2020. By enabling any tier-2 or tier-3 engineering college to install an offline, sub-\$500 edge appliance in lecture halls, institutions can automatically generate standardized, bilingual textbook-grade monographs and synchronized video subtitles. Vernacular-medium students can read explanations in their mother tongue while mastering international mathematical notation, fundamentally lowering attrition rates in challenging STEM curricula.

\section{Limitations and Future Research Directions}
While EdgeLectureAI achieves its design objectives, several future research avenues remain:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Multimodal Whiteboard Fusion:} Integrating a lightweight edge vision transformer to synchronize lecturer blackboard handwritten chalk strokes with the spoken audio stream.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Speaker Diarization:} Extending the VAD pipeline with clustered speaker embeddings to distinguish between professor exposition and student questions.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Lingual Indic Expansion:} Extending the translation engine beyond Hindi to include Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, and Marathi using parameter-efficient Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA).
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis has designed, implemented, and empirically validated \textbf{EdgeLectureAI}, an autonomous, edge-native system for real-time mathematical speech-to-LaTeX transcription and bilingual monograph synthesis deployed on the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano. By bridging the semantic gap between verbal discourse and structured LaTeX syntax, the system overcomes the critical failure modes of conventional conversational ASR.

Operating under a strict 15\,W power envelope and within an 8\,GB unified memory boundary, the dual-daemon architecture delivers an ASR throughput of $18.2\times$ real-time, sub-second SLM mathematical formatting, and sub-6-second XeTeX compilation. Through the formal implementation of the \textit{Mathematical Invariant Preservation Principle}, the system autonomously synthesizes publication-grade bilingual English and Hindi monographs with identical structural and mathematical parity. The successful end-to-end processing of full-length academic lectures establishes EdgeLectureAI as a practical, sovereign, and transformative tool for advancing equitable technical education.
