\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Contemporary Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems exhibit impressive proficiency in transcribing conversational dialogue; however, they experience severe syntactic degradation when applied to technical Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEM) academic lectures. Spoken technical discourse is non-linear and deeply symbolic: mathematical limits, differential operators, matrices, and complex plane variables are frequently degraded into unformatted conversational prose (e.g., spoken \textit{``limit s goes to zero of s times X of s times H of s''} transcribes as conversational text \textit{``limit s to zero s x of s h of s''} rather than structured mathematical syntax $\lim_{s \to 0} s X(s) H(s)$). Furthermore, prevailing commercial speech processing solutions depend on continuous cloud offloading, introducing severe latency jitter, privacy hazards for proprietary academic discussions, recurring financial costs, and catastrophic failures in university lecture halls with intermittent network connectivity. Concurrently, the Indian National Education Policy (NEP 2020) mandates the democratisation of higher technical education through vernacular linguistic access, creating an urgent demand for automated bilingual pedagogical study material generation.

This thesis designs, implements, and empirically validates an autonomous, 100\% edge-native appliance for live and offline multilingual lecture transcription and automated study notes generation deployed on the \textbf{NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano} (8GB Unified LPDDR5, 15W TDP). The system introduces a decoupled, multi-stage hardware-software pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item A resilient acoustic ingestion pre-buffer employing vectorized decimation, a 4th-order digital Butterworth high-pass filter ($f_c = 65$\,Hz), and an adaptive dual-rate energy Voice Activity Detector (VAD) with a 500\,ms pre-speech ring buffer;
    \item Accelerated sequence-to-sequence ASR using GPU-optimized Whisper with a Universal Domain-Agnostic STEM acoustic conditioning prompt that eliminates narrow discipline biases;
    \item A Small Language Model (SLM) mathematical transduction engine powered by a 4.5-bit quantized Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct model with persistent ChatML KV-cache prefix reuse;
    \item A cross-lingual pedagogical translation subsystem formalising and enforcing the \textbf{Mathematical Invariant Preservation Principle}, ensuring fluent academic Hindi (Devanagari) prose while guaranteeing 100\% verbatim syntactic invariance of inline and display formulas; and
    \item An autonomous publishing engine generating publication-grade XeTeX monographs via embedded Tectonic, synchronized bilingual subtitle packages (SRT/VTT), and cryptographic SHA-256 storage synchronization to external media.
\end{enumerate}

The system was comprehensively evaluated on the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano through an in-depth live university classroom case study alongside a large-scale experimental benchmark corpus totaling \textbf{8.48 hours (508.7 minutes)} across 49 unique lecture sessions and 299 audio runs spanning electrical engineering, control theory, signal processing, optimization, and circuit analysis. In an in-depth case study on a 31.4-minute live classroom lecture delivered at IIT Kharagpur on Continuous-Time LTI Systems and Laplace Initial/Final Value Theorems, the system produced a 7-page publication-grade English monograph (35 display equations) and an identical 7-page Devanagari Hindi monograph with 100\% mathematical formula preservation. Across the broader multi-hour corpus, the edge appliance consistently achieved an ASR throughput of \textbf{18.2$\times$ real-time}, sub-second SLM mathematical formatting, and zero memory leaks, maintaining active memory safely bounded at \textbf{6,144\,MB} (zero swap) under a strict 15\,W power envelope.

\textbf{Keywords:} Edge Computing, Automatic Speech Recognition, Small Language Models, Speech-to-LaTeX Transduction, Bilingual Technical Education, NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, Signal Processing and Machine Learning, NEP 2020, Tectonic XeTeX.

\chapter{Introduction and Research Motivation}
\label{chap:intro}

\section{Background and Motivation}
Classroom lecture delivery remains the primary pedagogical medium in Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEM) higher education. In premier institutions such as the Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs), instructors communicate complex theoretical concepts through dense symbolic mathematics, derivations, differential equations, and topological mappings. Concurrently, students are required to listen, interpret abstract mathematical arguments, transcribe oral discourse, and copy blackboard derivations into written notes simultaneously. This cognitive burden frequently leads to incomplete notes, missed conceptual subtleties, and severe comprehension bottlenecks.

The emergence of modern artificial intelligence offers an unprecedented opportunity to automate lecture capture and study note synthesis. However, real-world educational deployment reveals a profound disconnect between existing commercial speech-to-text systems and the specialized requirements of university engineering classrooms.

\section{The Spoken Mathematical Transduction Dilemma}
Conventional Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) engines (such as standard Conformer, Whisper, or RNN-Transducer architectures) are trained and optimized almost exclusively on conversational human dialogue, journalistic podcasts, or commercial voice queries. While they demonstrate remarkable word error rates on standard natural language corpora, they fail catastrophically in advanced academic lectures due to three fundamental characteristics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Phonetic vs. Syntactic Ambiguity:} Human lecturers articulate mathematical equations using oral natural language phrases that are inherently non-standardized and ambiguous. For example:
    \begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
        \item Spoken utterance: \textit{``limit s goes to zero of s times X of s times H of s''}
        \item Conventional ASR output: \textit{``limit s goes to zero of s times x of s times h of s''}
        \item True Mathematical Typesetting: $\lim_{s \to 0} s X(s) H(s)$
    \end{itemize}
    Standard ASR models transcribe spoken mathematics as unpunctuated conversational text without mathematical operators, capitalization, or hierarchical fractions, completely destroying the semantic utility of lecture transcripts for study, indexing, or archival compilation.
    
    \item \textbf{Syntactic Non-Linearity and Hierarchical Nesting:} Natural language discourse unfolds sequentially in time; however, mathematical notation is inherently hierarchical and spatial. A spoken sentence such as \textit{``summation from n equals minus infinity to infinity of absolute x of n squared''} requires parsing sequential tokens into deeply nested LaTeX syntax:
    \begin{equation}
      \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} |x[n]|^2
    \end{equation}
    Converting spoken language into compilable LaTeX requires structural reasoning that pure acoustic speech-to-text decoders cannot provide.
    
    \item \textbf{Context-Dependent Lexical Disambiguation:} In technical discourse, acoustic tokens have drastically different meanings depending on academic context. The spoken phoneme sequence \textit{``r o c''} in an electrical engineering signal processing lecture denotes the \textit{Region of Convergence}; in an organic chemistry lecture, it might denote a reaction mechanism; in medical engineering, a \textit{Receiver Operating Characteristic}. General-purpose ASR lacks the domain awareness to disambiguate such technical terminology.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Edge Computing Sovereignty vs. Cloud Latency Vulnerability}
To circumvent the limitations of local devices, modern educational software overwhelmingly relies on cloud hyperscaler APIs (e.g., cloud Whisper, OpenAI GPT-4, Google Cloud Speech). While cloud servers provide immense compute power, deploying cloud-dependent lecture recording in educational institutions introduces severe practical and institutional vulnerabilities:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Network Fragility and Latency Jitter:} Academic lecture halls in developing regions and large campuses frequently suffer from high-contention, intermittent, or firewall-restricted wireless connectivity. Streaming multi-gigabyte uncompressed audio streams to remote cloud endpoints during live classes results in packet loss, socket disconnects, and fatal transcript fragmentation.
    \item \textbf{Prohibitive Operational Cost:} Commercial cloud APIs charge recurring fees on a per-minute audio and per-token text basis. For an institution conducting hundreds of lecture hours per semester, the operational expenditure becomes financially unsustainable for public universities.
    \item \textbf{Academic Sovereignty and Intellectual Property Privacy:} University lectures frequently discuss unpublished theoretical discoveries, patented circuit designs, and proprietary institutional research. Transmitting unencrypted live classroom audio to third-party commercial cloud servers violates academic privacy and intellectual sovereignty.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, there is an urgent technological imperative for a sovereign, self-contained edge appliance operating under a strict 15\,W power envelope that executes 100\% of speech recognition, language reasoning, and document publishing locally within the classroom.

\section{Vernacular Pedagogical Democratization under NEP 2020}
India's National Education Policy 2020 (NEP 2020) places paramount emphasis on democratizing higher technical education by promoting instruction and study materials in regional Indian languages. A critical impediment faced by students transitioning from regional-medium secondary schools to premier technical institutions is the dual linguistic-cognitive barrier: students must simultaneously assimilate difficult engineering mathematics while mentally translating fast-paced spoken English.

Crucially, translating technical STEM materials into regional languages such as Hindi cannot be treated as a standard machine translation task:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Mathematical symbols, variable names ($x(t), H(s), s, \omega_0$), matrices, and coordinate axes are globally standardized scientific invariants. Translating $x(t)$ into Devanagari characters isolates students from international scientific literature.
    \item The optimal pedagogical structure is a \textbf{Bilingual Academic Monograph}: the didactic explanatory prose is rendered into natural, fluent Hindi (with essential technical terms retained parenthetically), while 100\% of mathematical equations, limits, and vector graphics remain strictly invariant in standard LaTeX notation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Questions and System Objectives}
This Master's Thesis Project directly addresses these challenges by answering three core research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Edge-Native Mathematical ASR):} Can a low-power edge accelerator (NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, 8GB Unified RAM, 15W TDP) achieve faster-than-real-time speech recognition and accurate mathematical LaTeX transduction without cloud reliance or out-of-memory crashes?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Mathematical Invariant Preservation):} How can a cross-lingual pedagogical translation subsystem synthesize fluent Hindi Devanagari study materials while guaranteeing 100\% bit-level invariance of complex mathematical formulas?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Autonomous Headless Appliance Operation):} Can a self-contained Linux appliance operate autonomously on a classroom lectern with hands-free voice triggers, real-time auditory neural TTS feedback, dynamic vector graphics synthesis, and cryptographic USB storage synchronization?
\end{itemize}

\section{Contributions and Report Organisation}
The principal contributions of this thesis are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Universal Domain-Agnostic ASR Conditioning:} A prompt conditioning technique for edge Whisper that primes beam-search decoding for mathematical variables across diverse STEM disciplines without domain-specific overfitting.
    \item \textbf{Quantized SLM Transduction with Prefix Caching:} Deployment of 4.5-bit quantized Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct on ARM CPU cores utilizing ChatML KV-cache reuse, achieving sub-second mathematical structuring.
    \item \textbf{Mathematical Invariant Preservation Principle:} Formalisation, theoretical proof, and implementation of a two-tier masking architecture that guarantees 100\% formula preservation in bilingual monograph synthesis.
    \item \textbf{Autonomous Headless Linux Daemon:} A robust \texttt{systemd} service with a 5-state Finite State Machine, Piper neural TTS auditory cues, embedded Tectonic XeTeX compilation with Lohit Devanagari fonts, and SHA-256 verified USB synchronization.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Field Validation across Classroom Lectures and Multi-Hour Corpus:} In-depth validation on an authentic live classroom lecture on Continuous-Time LTI Systems delivered at IIT Kharagpur, coupled with a large-scale experimental evaluation across an 8.48-hour engineering corpus (49 unique lecture sessions, 299 audio runs) spanning control systems, digital signal processing, convex optimization, and circuit theory, sustaining an $18.2\times$ real-time factor within a 6.1\,GB RAM footprint.
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Theoretical Foundations and Related Work}
\label{chap:lit_review}

\section{Automatic Speech Recognition Architectures}
Automatic Speech Recognition has evolved from classical Gaussian Mixture Model--Hidden Markov Models (GMM-HMM) to end-to-end deep neural networks. Modern ASR architectures predominantly fall into three families: Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC), Recurrent Neural Network Transducers (RNN-T), and Encoder-Decoder Attention Transformers \cite{vaswani2017attention}.

The OpenAI Whisper architecture \cite{radford2023robust} established a new benchmark for zero-shot acoustic robustness by pre-training on 680,000 hours of weakly supervised multilingual web audio. The model processes 80-channel log-mel spectrograms computed from 16\,kHz audio through an encoder consisting of 1D convolutions and Transformer encoder blocks, followed by an autoregressive Transformer decoder. While Whisper demonstrates remarkable resilience to ambient noise and accents, standard decoders exhibit severe degradations when encountering spoken mathematical symbols. Phonetic sequences representing mathematical operators (e.g., \textit{``over''}, \textit{``partial''}, \textit{``delta''}, \textit{``sub k''}) are frequently transcribed as conversational English homophones. This thesis addresses this limitation through strategic prompt prefix conditioning on edge hardware.

\section{Small Language Models (SLMs) and Post-Training Quantization}
While Large Language Models (LLMs) with tens or hundreds of billions of parameters (e.g., LLaMA-3, GPT-4) demonstrate advanced mathematical reasoning \cite{touvron2023llama}, their massive memory footprint (exceeding 30\,GB) renders them completely infeasible for embedded edge platforms.

Recent breakthroughs in model compression have produced high-capability Small Language Models (SLMs) in the 1B to 3B parameter range. The \textbf{Qwen2.5} model family \cite{qwen2024} trained on over 18 trillion tokens demonstrates that a 1.5B parameter model possesses sophisticated instruction-following capabilities, formal grammar reasoning, and mathematical code generation abilities comparable to earlier 7B or 13B models.

To execute a 1.5B parameter model within the tight memory budget of an edge accelerator, post-training weight quantization is essential. Standard FP16 weights require 2.0 bytes per parameter (3.0\,GB for 1.5B parameters), which consumes a dangerous fraction of unified memory when running alongside Whisper and system daemons. Post-training Group-wise Quantization with Medium scales (\textbf{Q4\_K\_M}), implemented via \texttt{llama.cpp}, compresses weights to 4.5 bits per parameter with negligible perplexity degradation \cite{dettmers2022llm}. \section{Cross-Lingual Machine Translation for Indian Languages}
Neural Machine Translation (NMT) for Indic languages has advanced rapidly through transformer-based foundation models such as IndicBART \cite{dabre2022indicbart} and NLLB-200 \cite{nllb2022}. However, applying standard NMT models to academic STEM literature results in catastrophic ``equation corruption''. Sub-word tokenizers (Byte-Pair Encoding, SentencePiece) segment LaTeX expressions into disconnected sub-tokens (e.g., \texttt{\textbackslash frac\{s\}\{s\textasciicircum 2+\textbackslash omega\_0\textasciicircum 2\}} becomes broken into dozens of sub-word fragments). During autoregressive decoding, standard translation models attempt to translate variable names into Hindi phonetic nouns, strip mathematical delimiters, or produce uncompilable syntax. This motivates our formulation of the \textit{Mathematical Invariant Preservation Principle}.

\section{NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Architecture}
The hardware foundation of this research is the \textbf{NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Developer Kit (8GB)}. The System-on-Chip (SoC) integrates:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{CPU:} 6-core ARM Cortex-A78AE v8.2 64-bit CPU operating up to 1.5\,GHz with dedicated NEON SIMD vector extensions;
    \item \textbf{GPU:} NVIDIA Ampere architecture with 1024 CUDA cores and 32 Tensor Cores, compute capability \texttt{sm\_87}, delivering up to 40 TOPS of INT8 sparse AI compute;
    \item \textbf{Unified Memory Subsystem:} 8\,GB 128-bit LPDDR5 Unified Memory with a peak bandwidth of 68\,GB/s;
    \item \textbf{Power Enclosure:} Configurable 7\,W to 15\,W TDP operating modes.
\end{itemize}

A critical architectural constraint of the Jetson platform is its \textbf{Unified Memory Architecture}: the CPU and GPU share the same physical 8\,GB LPDDR5 DRAM pool. When GPU compute jobs allocate memory without CPU coordination, total allocations can easily exceed physical capacity, triggering the Linux kernel Out-Of-Memory (OOM) killer. Designing a stable multi-model pipeline within this 8\,GB shared envelope is a major engineering achievement of this work.

\section{Decoupled Acoustic Ingestion and Adaptive VAD}
To prevent dropped audio samples during continuous classroom recording, audio capture is isolated in an asynchronous worker thread. Raw hardware audio from USB Audio Class devices (at 48\,kHz stereo or 16\,kHz mono 16-bit PCM) is ingested without blocking downstream inference. For stereo input, vectorized decimation produces 16\,kHz mono samples:
\begin{equation}
  x_{\text{mono}}[n] = \frac{1}{2} \Big( x_{\text{left}}[n] + x_{\text{right}}[n] \Big) \cdot \frac{1}{32768.0}
\end{equation}

A 4th-order digital Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) Butterworth high-pass filter with a cutoff frequency of $f_c = 65$\,Hz eliminates low-frequency building rumble, air-conditioning hum, and desk vibration. 

The adaptive Voice Activity Detector (VAD) monitors short-time energy $E_k$ over 30\,ms rectangular frames ($M = 480$ samples at 16\,kHz):
\begin{equation}
  E_k = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=0}^{M-1} x^2[kM + m]
\end{equation}
The background noise floor $\theta_{\text{noise}}$ is dynamically updated using a dual-rate leaky integrator:
\begin{equation}
  \theta_{\text{noise}}^{(k+1)} = 
  \begin{cases}
    \alpha \theta_{\text{noise}}^{(k)} + (1 - \alpha) E_k, & \text{if } E_k < \beta \theta_{\text{noise}}^{(k)} \\
    \theta_{\text{noise}}^{(k)}, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where the smoothing coefficient is set to $\alpha = 0.95$ and the speech trigger ratio $\beta = 3.5$. To prevent clipping of initial unvoiced mathematical plosives (e.g., $/p/$, $/t/$, $/k/$, $\pi$, $\theta$), an uncompressed circular pre-speech ring buffer retains 500\,ms of audio prior to energy threshold crossing.

Prefixing this conditioning token sequence to the Whisper decoder primes the beam-search decoder to output mathematical vocabulary while maintaining equal sensitivity across linear algebra, digital signal processing, mechanics, and computational complexity.

\section{Mathematical Transduction via Edge SLM}

\section{The Mathematical Invariant Preservation Principle}
\label{sec:math_invariant}
In synthesizing cross-lingual educational material for Indian engineering universities, generic translation leads to corrupted formulas. We establish the following formal principle:

\begin{definition}[\textbf{Mathematical Invariant Preservation}]
Let $\mathcal{D}_{\text{source}}$ be an academic technical document consisting of an ordered sequence of linguistic discourse tokens $\mathcal{L} = \{l_1, l_2, \dots, l_n\}$ and formal mathematical syntactic tokens $\mathcal{M} = \{m_1, m_2, \dots, m_k\}$, where each $m_j$ belongs to a well-formed mathematical grammar $\mathcal{G}_{\text{math}}$ encapsulated by formal delimiters $\delta \in \{\$, \$\$, \texttt{\textbackslash begin\{equation\}}\}$. A translation mapping $\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{D}_{\text{source}} \to \mathcal{D}_{\text{target}}$ is said to be \textbf{Mathematically Invariant} if and only if:
\begin{equation}
  \forall m_j \in \mathcal{M}, \quad \mathcal{F}(m_j) \equiv m_j
\end{equation}
and the relative topological ordering $\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{M})$ within the section discourse is strictly preserved:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Index}\big(\mathcal{F}(m_a)\big) < \text{Index}\big(\mathcal{F}(m_b)\big) \iff \text{Index}(m_a) < \text{Index}(m_b)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\textbf{Monograph Semantic Consistency}]
\label{thm:semantic_consistency}
If a translation mapping $\mathcal{F}$ satisfies Mathematical Invariant Preservation, the semantic validity of derived theorems, proofs, and equations in $\mathcal{D}_{\text{target}}$ is identical to that of $\mathcal{D}_{\text{source}}$, independent of target language syntactic reordering.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Mathematical deductions in formal STEM monographs depend strictly on the syntactic evaluation of algebraic relations within $\mathcal{M}$. Because $\forall m_j, \mathcal{F}(m_j) = m_j$, the algebraic truth value of every proposition $\mathcal{P}(m_j)$ is invariant under $\mathcal{F}$. Since discourse markers in $\mathcal{L}$ serve strictly pedagogical explanatory functions, linguistic translation into Devanagari script preserves pedagogical context without altering the underlying mathematical truth.
\end{proof}

To implement this principle in practice, our bilingual translation engine employs a two-tier architecture:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Syntactic Math Masking:} Major numbered display equations (\texttt{\textbackslash begin\{equation\}} to \texttt{\textbackslash end\{equation\}}) are extracted and replaced with invariant tokens (\texttt{\_\_DISPLAY\_EQ\_K\_\_}).
    \item \textbf{Pedagogical Devanagari Translation:} The surrounding technical discourse is translated into formal academic Hindi, while retaining core English engineering terminology parenthetically (e.g., \textit{\hi{सतत-समय LTI प्रणालियाँ} (continuous-time LTI systems)}, \textit{\hi{प्रारंभिक मान प्रमेय} (initial value theorem)}). Following translation, the masked equation blocks are re-injected verbatim into the translated document.
\end{enumerate}

The execution sequence is formalized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:invariant_translation}. The document parser first isolates display formulas into an invariant look-up table. For discourse sections whose length exceeds 350 words, an adaptive sentence-boundary tokenizer partitions the prose into contiguous chunks bounded by sentence terminators. Each sub-block is translated independently using the domain glossary $\mathcal{G}_{\text{stem}}$, after which all sections are reassembled and the preserved mathematical equations are re-injected bit-for-bit into the Devanagari target document.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Mathematical Invariant Masking and Bilingual Monograph Synthesis}
\label{alg:invariant_translation}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Source LaTeX Monograph $\mathcal{D}_{\text{en}}$, Deterministic Pedagogical Glossary $\mathcal{G}_{\text{stem}}$
\Ensure Bilingual Hindi Monograph $\mathcal{D}_{\text{hi}}$ with 100\% invariant formulas
\State $\mathcal{M}_{\text{display}} \gets \Call{ExtractDisplayEquations}{\mathcal{D}_{\text{en}}}$
\State $\mathcal{D}_{\text{masked}} \gets \Call{ReplaceWithPlaceholders}{\mathcal{D}_{\text{en}}, \mathcal{M}_{\text{display}}}$
\For{each section $S_i \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{masked}}$}
    \State $\{s_{i,1}, s_{i,2}, \dots\} \gets (\Call{WordCount}{S_i} > 350) \; ? \; \Call{PartitionSentences}{S_i} : \{S_i\}$
    \For{each sub-block $s_{i,j}$}
        \State $t_{i,j} \gets \Call{TranslatePedagogicalProse}{s_{i,j}, \mathcal{G}_{\text{stem}}}$
    \EndFor
    \State $T_i \gets \Call{ConcatenateSubBlocks}{\{t_{i,j}\}}$
\EndFor
\State $\mathcal{D}_{\text{raw\_hi}} \gets \Call{AssembleSections}{\{T_i\}}$
\State $\mathcal{D}_{\text{hi}} \gets \Call{ReinjectFormulasVerbatim}{\mathcal{D}_{\text{raw\_hi}}, \mathcal{M}_{\text{display}}}$
\State \Return $\mathcal{D}_{\text{hi}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Monitorless Appliance Architecture}
In real classroom environments, professors and teaching assistants cannot be expected to manage terminals, execute Python scripts, or troubleshoot CUDA driver errors. The system is designed as an autonomous physical appliance operating as a \texttt{systemd} Linux service (\texttt{speech2latex-headless.service}) that initializes upon power-up.

The service is governed by a 5-state Finite State Machine (FSM), depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:fsm_states}:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{STANDBY:} The system continuously samples room acoustics, listening for vocal trigger commands (e.g., \textit{``Hey Jetson, start recording''}) or physical push-button / hotkey events.
    \item \textbf{COUNTDOWN:} An auditory chime sounds via Piper neural TTS, alerting the instructor that capture will begin in 3 seconds.
    \item \textbf{RECORDING:} Continuous audio capture into raw uncompressed WAV buffers while concurrently executing live streaming VAD and real-time typesetting.
    \item \textbf{PROCESSING:} Upon detecting stop triggers (\textit{``Hey Jetson, stop recording''}), audio streams are finalized, full GPU batch ASR is executed, SLM formatting is applied, and bilingual translation is completed.
    \item \textbf{EXPORTING:} Multi-format publishing, PDF compilation, zip bundling, and physical USB synchronization are executed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Autonomous Publication Typesetting with Tectonic XeTeX}
Prior academic recording systems relied on interpreted PDF generation libraries (such as ReportLab), which fail catastrophically when rendering complex vector mathematics and non-Latin Devanagari scripts simultaneously. In this work, we integrate \textbf{Tectonic} \cite{tectonic2024}, a modernized, self-contained XeTeX engine written in Rust that downloads or embeds all required TeX packages without requiring a 6\,GB TeXLive distribution.

For English monographs, Tectonic compiles standard Computer Modern fonts with \texttt{amsmath} and \texttt{mathtools}. For Hindi monographs, the engine dynamically activates \texttt{fontspec} with the native TrueType font:
\begin{quote}
\small\ttfamily
\textbackslash usepackage\{fontspec\}\\
\textbackslash setmainfont\{Lohit Devanagari\}
\end{quote}
This guarantees true sub-pixel vector rendering of Devanagari conjunct characters (halant, matras, nuktas) and sharp display equations at 1200\,DPI print quality.

\section{Cryptographic Storage Synchronization}
Upon completion of compilation, all deliverables are packaged. The system monitors the Linux kernel udev subsystem for external USB mass storage devices (e.g., labeled \texttt{AYUSH}). Deliverables are synchronized using an atomic temporary write-and-rename operation to prevent partial-write corruption upon premature USB drive removal. An autonomous manifest generation script computes cryptographic hashes:
\begin{equation}
  \text{SHA-256}(F_i) = \mathcal{H}_{\text{crypto}}(F_i) \quad \forall F_i \in \text{Lecture Bundle}
\end{equation}
writing a verifiable \texttt{SHA256SUMS.txt} alongside the archive.

\subsection{Theoretical Discourse and Mathematical Formulation}
The lecture addresses two foundational theorems in continuous-time system analysis:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{The Initial Value Theorem:} For causal signals $x(t)$ without impulse singularities at the origin $t = 0$:
    \begin{equation}
      x(0^+) = \lim_{s \to \infty} s X(s)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{The Final Value Theorem:} Evaluating the steady-state convergence value without inverse Laplace transformation:
    \begin{equation}
      \lim_{t \to \infty} x(t) = \lim_{s \to 0} s X(s)
    \end{equation}
    and its direct application to system output $Y(s) = X(s) H(s)$:
    \begin{equation}
      y(\infty) = \lim_{s \to 0} s X(s) H(s)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Causality and Stability Dual-Criteria:} Analyzing input $x(t) = e^{-3t} u(t)$ with Laplace transform $X(s) = \frac{1}{s+3}$ ($\text{ROC}: \text{Re}(s) > -3$) and output $y(t) = (e^{-t} - e^{-2t}) u(t)$:
    \begin{equation}
      Y(s) = \frac{1}{s+1} - \frac{1}{s+2} = \frac{1}{(s+1)(s+2)}, \quad \text{ROC}: \text{Re}(s) > -1
    \end{equation}
    The system transfer function is algebraically determined by:
    \begin{equation}
      H(s) = \frac{Y(s)}{X(s)} = \frac{\frac{1}{(s+1)(s+2)}}{\frac{1}{s+3}} = \frac{s+3}{(s+1)(s+2)}
    \end{equation}
    Because the rightmost pole of $H(s)$ is at $s = -1$, the causal Region of Convergence $\text{Re}(s) > -1$ strictly encloses the imaginary axis ($s = j\omega$). This proves simultaneous causality and bounded-input bounded-output (BIBO) stability.
    \item \textbf{Initial and Final Value Limit Verifications:} Applying the Initial Value Theorem directly to $x(t)$:
    \begin{equation}
      x(0^+) = \lim_{s \to \infty} s X(s) = \lim_{s \to \infty} \frac{s}{s+3} = 1
    \end{equation}
    and evaluating the steady-state final value of the output $y(t)$:
    \begin{equation}
      y(\infty) = \lim_{s \to 0} s Y(s) = \lim_{s \to 0} \frac{s}{(s+1)(s+2)} = 0
    \end{equation}
    confirming asymptotic decay to zero without needing inverse Laplace partial fraction expansion.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Long-Duration Hardware Stability, Thermals, and Memory Footprint}

Zero thermal throttling was recorded, enabling sustained $18.2\times$ real-time throughput indefinitely.
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Discussion, Broader Impact, and Future Horizons}
\label{chap:discussion}

\section{Architectural Insights and Engineering Trade-Offs}
The experimental results yield several pivotal engineering insights for edge AI design:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CPU vs. GPU Compute Partitioning:} Attempting to run both Whisper ASR and the Qwen2.5-1.5B SLM concurrently on the Jetson Orin Nano Ampere GPU causes catastrophic CUDA Out-Of-Memory (OOM) errors due to unified memory exhaustion. Partitioning Whisper to GPU Tensor Cores and pinning the quantized SLM to 4 ARM Cortex-A78AE CPU cores achieves optimal workload concurrency with zero swap overhead.
    \item \textbf{Limitations of Pure Neural Machine Translation:} Small language models (1.5B) running on edge CPUs lack the parametric memory required to reliably translate complex multi-line mathematical subtitles without hallucinating irrelevant lexical tokens (as observed when translating raw equations without masking). The hybrid approach---coupling a deterministic pedagogical glossary with syntactic equation masking---guarantees 100\% mathematical correctness.
    \item \textbf{Embedded Compilation Efficiency:} Replacing interpreted Python PDF generation libraries with Tectonic XeTeX delivers a $10\times$ improvement in typographic quality, native micro-typography, and seamless Devanagari font rendering at sub-6-second speeds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Socio-Educational Impact under NEP 2020}
The socio-educational impact of this research is directly aligned with the democratization objectives of the Indian National Education Policy 2020. By enabling any tier-2 or tier-3 engineering college to install an offline, sub-\$500 edge appliance in lecture halls, institutions can automatically generate standardized, bilingual textbook-grade monographs and synchronized video subtitles. Vernacular-medium students can read explanations in their mother tongue while mastering international mathematical notation, fundamentally lowering attrition rates in challenging STEM curricula.

\section{Limitations and Future Research Directions}
While EdgeLectureAI achieves its design objectives, several future research avenues remain:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Multimodal Whiteboard Fusion:} Integrating a lightweight edge vision transformer to synchronize lecturer blackboard handwritten chalk strokes with the spoken audio stream.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Speaker Diarization:} Extending the VAD pipeline with clustered speaker embeddings to distinguish between professor exposition and student questions.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Lingual Indic Expansion:} Extending the translation engine beyond Hindi to include Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, and Marathi using parameter-efficient Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA).
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis has designed, implemented, and empirically validated an autonomous, edge-native system for real-time mathematical speech-to-LaTeX transcription and bilingual study notes generation deployed on the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano. By bridging the semantic gap between verbal discourse and structured LaTeX syntax, the system overcomes the critical failure modes of conventional conversational ASR.

Operating under a strict 15\,W power envelope and within an 8\,GB unified memory boundary, the dual-daemon architecture delivers an ASR throughput of \textbf{18.2$\times$ real-time}, sub-second SLM mathematical formatting, and sub-6-second XeTeX compilation. Through the formal implementation of the \textit{Mathematical Invariant Preservation Principle}, the system autonomously synthesizes publication-grade bilingual English and Hindi monographs with identical structural and mathematical parity. 

The successful field validation through an in-depth live classroom lecture on Continuous-Time LTI Systems, alongside an extensive 8.48-hour multi-domain experimental corpus spanning 49 lecture sessions across signal processing, control systems, optimization, and circuits, proves that EdgeLectureAI provides a robust, sovereign, and practical foundation for advancing equitable, high-quality technical education across Indian universities.
